Doris Duke's butler reportedly earned about $40,000 a year. Then her will put him in charge of her $1.2 billion fortune.

The New York Times called him "a barely literate man with a drinking problem."

In the video below, the judge who heard the case and people who knew Duke tell the story.

Bernard Lafferty, born in Ireland, was hired in 1987. Six months before Duke died in October 1993, at 80, she named him executor.

The will gave him a $5 million fee and $500,000 a year for life.

Her former cook said: "He made it clear that everyone had to go through him first to speak to Miss Duke about anything."

Court records said he bought $20,000 worth of clothes on her accounts in October 1993 alone.

Then came about 40 lawyers from 10 firms. Prosecutors looked into claims he hastened her death. No charges were filed.

In 1996 he gave up control and kept $4.5 million plus the $500,000 a year. "The reason why I step aside is because it will let the money go to the charities that Miss Duke wanted," he said.

He died that November, at 51.

He was promised $500,000 a year for the rest of his life. The rest of his life lasted 3 years.

Was Doris Duke's butler her most loyal friend, or the luckiest man in America?
