Liberace's chauffeur sued him for $113 million. He settled for $95,000, three cars and three dogs.

Scott Thorson was 22 when Liberace dismissed him in 1982. He said he had been the pianist's live-in lover for five years. His lawsuit asked for $113 million in palimony.

Liberace denied it. In 1984 depositions he insisted Thorson was never his lover.

The case ended out of court in 1986. Thorson said later that he settled because he knew Liberace was dying.

In 1986 he told The Tonight Show he owned about six homes. He said he put his money in real estate, not stocks or oil. In the video below, he shows them off.

He died on February 4, 1987, at 67. His press agent said anemia, emphysema and heart disease. The coroner said it was pneumonia linked to AIDS, and that the doctor had given a false cause.

At his death he was said to be worth $110 million. $88 million went to his foundation for scholarships.

The case for Thorson. Five years at Liberace's side, and he left with a tiny slice of what he asked for.

Do the math. He asked for $113 million. The $95,000 in cash was less than 0.1% of that.

Thorson asked for $113 million and got three dogs. The scholarship fund got $88 million.

Did Thorson settle for too little, or was he paid more than a denied claim was worth?
