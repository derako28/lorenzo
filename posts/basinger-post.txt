A jury ruled that Kim Basinger had made an oral agreement to star in a film. Then it ordered her to pay more than $8 million for walking away.

"Lost everything. Every single thing."

In the video below, Barbara Walters asks her about it in 1998.

The film was Boxing Helena. Madonna had dropped out, and Basinger stepped in. She asked for major script changes, didn't get them, and left.

The case for Main Line: she gave her word, and it argued it lost over $6 million in sales when she left.

The case for Basinger: there was no clear written contract, and in 1994 an appeals court set the verdict aside.

She filed for bankruptcy in 1993. Four years earlier she was reported to have bought the town of Braselton, Georgia, for $20 million. On the tape she says: "I never owned a town. I don't have $20 million. I never did."

Do the math. A verdict of more than $8 million. A settlement of $3.8 million.

A town she says she never owned. A film she never made.

Was a spoken yes really a contract, or did she have the right to walk away?
