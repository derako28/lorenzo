Cher left Sonny Bono in 1975 with $5,000 a month. The company with her name on it, she says, was 95% his and 5% his lawyer's.

She says she could not sign a check without Sonny's or his lawyer's signature. Her contract gave her three weeks of vacation a year and a salary she was "likely never paid."

In the video below, Cher tells Howard Stern the question she kept asking Sonny: what time of "the day or the night" he decided to "take Cher's money."

His answer, in her telling: "I knew you'd always leave me." She says she told him that was not good enough.

She says she reminded him: "I was there with you every day for 12 years." She was there when they crashed, she says, and when they borrowed money from their own chauffeur because they had nothing.

When they divorced, she says Sonny offered her whatever she wanted. She took her own place in Malibu and $5,000 a month.

In 2024, 26 years after Sonny died, she told The New York Times: "He took all my money."

The case for Cher. She was by his side for 12 years, in an act with her name on it.

The case for Sonny. He built the business, and on paper she was his employee.

Her name was on the company. The company wasn't hers.

Did Sonny owe Cher half, or was it his company to keep?
