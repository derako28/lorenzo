Bing Crosby's "White Christmas" sold an estimated 50 million copies, the best-selling single ever. His will said his 4 oldest sons couldn't touch their inheritance until they turned 65.

Only 1 of them lived that long.

Real footage below: 1954, Bing at home with Edward R. Murrow. Asked if it's tough raising 4 boys alone: "Sometimes I get so I hate to hear the phone ring."

Their mother, Dixie Lee, died in 1952 at 42. Bing died in 1977. His sons' share went into a blind trust, locked until 65. The reason, reportedly: to keep them out of trouble.

Until then, the sons lived on a trust their mother left. It was invested in oil stocks. In December 1989, after the oil glut, it folded.

Do the math.
Lindsay died that year at 51. 14 years short of 65.
Dennis died in 1991 at 56. 9 years short.
Gary died in 1995 at 62. 3 years short.
Only Phillip made it. He died in 2004 at 69.

In 2021, Bing's estate sold a stake in his rights in a deal estimated at more than $50 million.

Their father left them money they couldn't touch. Their mother left them money that ran out.

Was the 65 rule protecting his sons, or punishing them?
